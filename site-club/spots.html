Where to ride

A rider-built guide to launching a jetsurf — wherever you ride. It started with our home turf in South Florida, but it's open to riders anywhere. What to check before you go, and the rules that get enforced. Ride somewhere good? Send it to us, no matter where in the world it is, and we'll add it so the next rider doesn't have to guess.

Rider-submitted spots

Every spot here came from someone in the community, wherever they ride. Each one has its own trade-offs — parking, ramp vs. beach carry, how protected the water is, and how much boat traffic you'll share it with. Confirm access and posted rules on the day; they change.

Flat water · all levels

Miami Marine Stadium

Virginia Key, Miami, FL

The basin on Virginia Key — sheltered on nearly every wind, glassy first thing, and Miami's go-to flat-water spot. Boat ramp and paid trailer parking on site. Busy with wake boats and jet skis on weekends; keep it slow near the ramp and the seawall.

Fresh water · dedicated PWC area

Markham Park — PWC Launch Area

Sunrise, FL

A man-made lake at Markham Park in Sunrise, set aside specifically for personal watercraft — one of the few places in Broward you can legally open it up. Fresh water, so there's no salt to flush afterward. Paved launch, parking, and restrooms; the park charges an entrance fee on weekends and holidays. Busy and choppy with jet skis by midday — go early.

Fresh water · best for learning

Lake Ida West Park

Delray Beach, FL

Part of the Delray Beach chain of lakes — PWC-friendly water with a boat ramp and parking at 1455 Lake Ida Rd. Glassy first thing, which makes it the best spot on this list to learn on or teach someone, electric board or eFoil. Wake boats and jet skis show up as the morning goes on.

Salt water · ramp launch

Bill Bird Marina — Haulover

Miami Beach, FL

The county ramp at Haulover Park, 10800 Collins Ave, open 24 hours. Direct shot to the Haulover sandbar just south and out Haulover Inlet to the ocean. Salt water — flush the board the same day. Ramp fee, and it's one of the busiest ramps in the country on weekends. The inlet runs rough with wind against tide; it's no place for a first session.

Salt water · ramp launch

Riviera Beach Marina Village

Riviera Beach, FL

The Palm Beach County gateway at 190 E 13th St — public ramp and parking straight into the Lake Worth Lagoon, with Peanut Island and its protected water minutes away. Salt water, so flush the board the same day. Ramp fee; busy on weekends and the lagoon fills with boat traffic by midday.

Fresh water · ramp launch

John Prince Park — Lake Osborne

Lake Worth Beach, FL

A four-ramp launch on Lake Osborne at 1996 Center Dr, open 6 am–9 pm. Fresh water — no salt to flush — on the Palm Beach chain of lakes, connected through to Lake Ida. Plenty of parking; the lake gets busy with wake boats and jet skis on weekends, so ride the mornings.

Fresh water · big open lake

Lake Lanier

Gainesville, GA

A 38,000-acre reservoir an hour north of Atlanta, and the busiest lake in Georgia. Plenty of ramps and parks around the shore, wide open water in the middle, and wake boats everywhere on a summer weekend — early mornings are the flat ones.

Fresh water · coves & open water

Lake Norman

Mooresville, NC

North Carolina's largest man-made lake, just north of Charlotte, with public ramps and marinas around most of it. Long coves for flat water, open sections that chop up in wind, and heavy boat traffic near the marinas in season.

Salt water · protected bay

Biscayne Bay & the Intracoastal

Miami, FL

The home water — protected bay and Intracoastal running the length of Miami, with ramps and sandbars all along it. Flat early, busy by midday, and manatee and idle-speed zones throughout. Salt water: flush the board the same day.

Fresh water · permit required

Lake George

Lake George, NY

Thirty-two miles of clear water in the Adirondacks, with public launches at the village end. Note the paperwork: the Lake George Park Commission runs its own registration and launch permit on top of state registration, plus a mandatory boat inspection. Sort it before you tow.

Lake Winnipesaukee

Laconia, NH

New Hampshire's big lake — 44 square miles, hundreds of islands and no shortage of public ramps. Sheltered bays for flat riding, open stretches that build a real chop with wind, and a short season that runs hard from June to September.

Chicago, IL

Great Lake water off Chicago, Michigan, Wisconsin and Indiana, with harbours and launches the whole way round. Glassy on a calm morning and genuinely rough when the wind gets up — check the marine forecast, not just the beach, before you launch.

Osage Beach, MO

1,100 miles of shoreline and endless coves in central Missouri, with ramps, marinas and lakefront everywhere. The main channel gets busy and choppy with cruisers on weekends; the coves stay flat and quiet.

Excelsior, MN

A maze of bays and channels west of Minneapolis with public accesses around the lake. Sheltered water in the bays, plenty of marinas, and a summer-only season — it freezes solid in winter.

Lake St. Clair

St. Clair Shores, MI

Wide, shallow water between Detroit and Ontario, with metroparks and public ramps along the Michigan shore. Shallow means it kicks up quickly in wind and flattens just as fast — pick your day and watch the freighter channel.

Fresh water · electric friendly

Lake Tahoe

Tahoe City, CA

Alpine water on the California–Nevada line, and the strictest rules on this list: electric boards are the safe bet, while combustion craft face tight noise and emission limits plus mandatory inspection for invasive species. Check with the TRPA before you plan a trip. Cold water year-round — wear a wetsuit.

Lake Havasu City, AZ

Warm desert water on the Arizona–California border, with launch ramps in town and river running north and south. Long riding season, big boat traffic on holiday weekends, and serious heat — carry more water than you think.

Lake Powell

Page, AZ

Red-rock canyons and 1,900 miles of shoreline across Utah and Arizona. Every vessel needs an aquatic invasive species inspection before launching, and the ramps move with the water level — check what's open before you drive.

Seattle, WA

Seattle's lake, with public ramps on both shores and sheltered water most days. Cold year-round, busy with sailing and rowing traffic, and easy to ride in the evening after work in summer.

Hood River, OR

The Gorge — famous for wind, which is why kiters love it and why you want a calm early morning. Launching on the Oregon side needs an Oregon aquatic invasive species permit, and current runs hard here, so know the river before you go out.

What to include

The more of this you send, the faster it goes up — but a name and a pin is enough to start.

  • Spot name — what riders call it
  • Location — city, region, and country, wherever it is
  • How you launch — beach carry, boat ramp, dock, seawall
  • Parking — free / paid / permit, how much, how far from the water
  • Water — protected or open, depth, and hazards (seagrass, rocks, sandbar, current, prop wash)
  • Best conditions — wind direction and time of day it actually works
  • Rules & access — no-wake zones, motorized bans, ramp fees, manatee zones
  • A map pin — a dropped location or a Google Maps link
  • Your name — if you want the credit on the card (optional)

We'll leave off exact coordinates for anything sensitive — tell us if a spot should stay vague or club-only.

Before you go

Check the conditions

A five-minute check saves a wasted drive or a bad session. Pull up a forecast and a tide table before you load the board.

Wind · Tide · Swell

Wind, tide & swell

Wind under ~10 kt is comfortable, 15 kt+ gets choppy. Watch the tide for exposed sandbars and current, and aim for morning — it's usually the cleanest swell. One stop covers all three.

Check conditions on Surfline →
Temperature

Water temp / wetsuit

Miami water rarely needs more than a shorty, and often nothing in summer. Cooler months and a windy evening — bring a top.

Timing

Time of day

Early is calm water and low traffic. Midday is chop and powerboats. Evening can glass off again but watch the light — you must be off the water before dark.

Traffic

Boat & swimmer traffic

Weekends and holidays are busy everywhere. Keep clear of swim areas, anchored boats, and anyone flying a dive flag.

Stay legal · South Florida example

Rules that matter

A motorized board is treated like a vessel on the water almost everywhere. These are the ones that get riders stopped or fined around Miami-Dade — the rules we know best, since that's where we ride. Riding somewhere else? The categories below are a good checklist, but the specifics — registration, speed zones, protected wildlife, ramp access — are set locally and will differ from Florida's.

This is general guidance, not legal advice, and it's specific to Florida. Boating rules change and vary by zone and by country. Confirm current regulations with the Florida FWC and Miami-Dade if you're riding here, or with your own local marine authority if you're riding elsewhere.

Paperwork

Registration

A motor-powered board may need to be registered as a vessel in Florida. Check your model's status with FWC and carry the paperwork.

Speed zones

Idle speed / no wake

Posted near marinas, bridges, residential canals, and mooring fields. Idle or slow speed, minimum wake, until you're past the last marker.

Wildlife

Manatee zones

Seasonal slow-speed and no-entry zones (commonly Nov 15–Mar 31) protect manatees in many Miami-Dade waters. Know the ones near your spot.

Navigation

Marked channels

Stay out of the main navigation channel. If you have to cross it, cross straight and quickly, and give way to bigger boats.

People in the water

Stay away from swimmers

This is the big one. A motorized board near people in the water is genuinely dangerous, and it's the fastest way to get stopped by the marine patrol or FWC — often before you've done anything else wrong. Keep a wide margin from swimmers, beaches, snorkelers and any displayed dive flag, and never ride through a designated swim area. If in doubt, go around.

Daylight

Night riding

Riding after sunset without proper navigation lights isn't allowed. Plan to be loaded up before dark.

Safety gear

Kill switch & leash

Always ride with the kill-switch magnet clipped and a leash on. A runaway board is a hazard to everyone around you.

Launch access

Where you put in

Some beaches and parks ban motorized craft or charge a ramp fee. Check the signage at the access point, not just the water.

After the session

Where to flush the board

Salt is what kills these boards. Rinse and flush the cooling system the same day — the maintenance guides cover how.

At the ramp

Ramp rinse stations

Some launches have a hose or a wash-down area. A quick exterior rinse before the board dries is better than nothing.
