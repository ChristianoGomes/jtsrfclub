Piston pin, connecting-rod bearing, washer orientation, ring alignment, and torquing the cylinder head to 7 Nm.

Guide

Piston removal & installation

Difficulty: Advanced Time: ~60 min Applies to: Gas boards Last updated: Sep 2026

Watch the walkthrough

Watch on YouTube ↗ · JETSURF Service Tutorials #10. Timestamps in the steps below follow the video's on-screen counter (0:44–4:34).

This is a rebuild job — piston, ring, small-end bearing. Every part here has an orientation that matters: the aluminum washers, the piston pin, the ring, and the clips. Work slowly and keep the crankcase covered. Cylinder head torque is 7 Nm.

Tools & supplies

  • Needle-nose pliers
  • Two-stroke oil (for lubrication on assembly)
  • Grease (combustion chamber cap)
  • Torque wrench (7 Nm)
  • New piston pin retaining clips
  • New piston ring and aluminum washers as needed
  • Clean rag to block the crankcase opening

Removal

  1. 0:44 — With needle-nose pliers, remove the retaining clip and tap the piston pin out. Do not let the clip drop into the crankcase.

Installation

  1. 0:55 — Lubricate the small end of the connecting rod, ideally with two-stroke oil.
  2. 1:07 — Fit the connecting-rod bearing. Lubricate the piston pin and both pin bores in the piston.
  3. 1:23 — Slide the bearing toward the connecting rod and set the aluminum washer next to it — flat side facing the bearing, rounded side toward the piston.
  4. 1:32 — Partially insert the piston pin from the side where the piston has the drilled hole for exhaust-port-divider lubrication.
  5. 1:40 — Place the second aluminum washer inside the piston, rounded side facing the piston.
  6. 1:53 — Slide the piston onto the connecting-rod bearing and push the pin fully through.
  7. 2:07 — Install the first retaining clip — always on the side where the piston ring locator pin is positioned.
  8. 2:27 — Install the second retaining clip.
  9. 2:30 — Fit the piston ring with the end mark facing up. The lubrication channel faces the exhaust-port side; the ring gap lock sits on the freewheel side.
  10. 2:47 — Lubricate the cylinder with oil and slide the piston into the cylinder.
  11. 3:31 — Tighten the cylinder bolts with a wrench, alternating in a crisscross pattern.
  12. 3:58 — Apply grease to the combustion chamber cap and set it onto the cylinder.
  13. 4:11 — Fit the cylinder head and start the six bolts.
  14. 4:34 — Tighten the head bolts to 7 Nm, in the same crisscross pattern.
Tip: get every orientation right before the pin goes through — washers (flat to bearing, rounded to piston), pin entry on the lube-hole side, ring end mark up, ring gap lock on the freewheel side. Once the clips are in, checking any of that means pulling it apart again.
Caution: stuff a clean rag into the crankcase opening before you touch the clips — a clip dropped into the crankcase means splitting the cases to get it out. Use new retaining clips every time, and don't exceed 7 Nm on the head bolts.
