Hand control / throttle cable — full replacement

Swapping a seized hand control — the whole handle-and-cable unit out through the snorkel and a new one drawn back through, wired, set and adjusted.

Guide

Hand control / throttle cable — full replacement

Difficulty: Advanced Time: half a day Applies to: Gas boards Last updated: Sep 2026

Watch the walkthrough

Watch on YouTube ↗ · Jetboard Australia — "JetSurf Control Handle Swap Made Easy, NO STRESS!" (~36 min). Timestamps below follow the video's own time. JETSURF's own version: Handle removal & installation.

The throttle cable is built into the hand control, so a seized cable means the whole handle unit gets replaced. The cable runs through the snorkel alongside the breather and the tether cord — the job is all about drawing the old one out and the new one back through without losing anything inside the board.

Why the whole control gets replaced — 2:37

  • 2:42 — The cable outer is bubbled and rusting — it still moves but stiffly, and it'll never come free enough to last. The cable is part of the hand control, so the control gets swapped.
  • 6:13 — Cause: 2.5 years old, no service, cable never lubricated (should be every 6–12 months), left with salt in the engine bay.

Remove the fuel tank & release the cable — 0:44

  1. 0:50 — Remove the fuel tank — strap, fuel line, fuel-pump cable (finger under the tab). Hold the hose, grab the tank, wriggle it out together; then pull the snap ring toward the tank and the breather tube pops out.
  2. 1:23 — Remove the cable clamp completely (Allen key) — note the groove the cable sits in.
  3. 1:49 — At the throttle drum, turn the throttle to full, drop the cable end down, and it lifts out of the slotted cylinder. Pull the cable through.
  4. 3:03 — Disconnect the wiring block (rubber-mounted — pull it toward you; a rubber tab holds it in a V-slot). The cable to trace is the one with the blue cable tie. Remove the O-ring holding the plug and pull it out.

Set up a draw cable — 4:24

  1. 4:38 — Tape a heavy wire (or string) to the handle knob with insulation tape — cover the edge so it can't catch, tapering it. Leave the tape a bit loose so you can get it off later.

Open the snorkel & free the breather — 5:20

  1. 5:40 — Undo the snorkel screws (odd-size Allen — pre-loosen; genuinely tight, sometimes you drill the heads and replace the rings). Pull the cord through a little and the snorkel loosens.
  2. 6:53 — Inside: the fuel-tank breather tube (note its position for reassembly) and the rubber sleeves — slide them along, screwdriver to help.
  3. 7:44 — Work the breather tube free (very tight). Do not drop it back into the board — tuck it aside.
  4. 8:34 — Pull the little elbow "duck's bill" ducting up out of the way — it makes threading the cable almost impossible if left in.

Pull the old assembly out — 9:00

  1. 9:06 — The plug sits in a rubber grommet (often popped off from corrosion — reglue later). Spread it to part it.
  2. 9:55 — Tie off any loose tube and tape it to the board so it can't disappear inside — that's the classic nightmare.
  3. 10:12 — Pull from the engine end, easing the plug through the small hole, until the whole assembly is out.
  4. 11:56 — Cut the old handle cord and cable ties. Melt the cut cord end with a torch so it can't fray, then feed the cord out through the three sleeves and the cable out of the snorkel.

Get the right hand control — 14:20

  • 14:25 — There are 3–4 versions by model. A DFI board has three plugs; 2016-era boards have a twin plug with different male/female positions. Order the exact control for your board — the wrong one simply won't work.

Feed the new control through — 14:57

  1. 15:04 — Feed the cable through the duck's bill first, then the snorkel.
  2. 15:24 — Tape the plug end well — the square, large-headed plug catches. Build a tape ramp so there's no abrupt edge. If you pull too hard and the tape strips, the cable detaches inside the board — a major job. Patience.
  3. 16:53 — Draw it back through working from both ends, watching the bulky joint. Feel for the tape through the rubber gland.

Reset the breather & snorkel — 18:30

  1. 18:38 — Slide the rubber sleeves back, feed the breather tube through, and the missing tether cord.
  2. 19:52 — Melt the breather end hard and push it through the three sleeves with a sharp scriber (mind your fingers).
  3. 21:26 — Position the breather about 20–30 mm inside the snorkel, just below its outlet.
  4. 22:39 — Refit the snorkel — line the screws up with the rubber and the cord with the board position, then the opposing screw.

Set the handle length — 23:33

  1. 23:33 — Even the cables up (personal preference — sets the "V" and the arc you ride with).
  2. 23:59 — For the length, Rod copies factory: pull it tight and it should sit just behind the two engine-cover latches, giving about 3 in / 70 mm of cable overlap in the middle.
  3. 24:50 — Tie it: around, over, through the back, back on itself into a loop — pull it square before tightening (a crooked handle is a crooked handle forever).
  4. 25:25 — Factory-style, add a couple of small cable ties to hold it. Fine-tune the loop so it doesn't catch your leg when riding.

Plug in the wiring — 26:53

  1. 27:05 — The blue-cable-tie lead is the 3-connector — it seats with the clip on top.
  2. 28:00 — The orange cable is the ignition coil — seat it in its slot.
  3. 28:24 — The O-ring goes over the top and latches, holding the whole block together.
  4. 28:48 — Slide the wiring block back up so its front rubber tab locks into the stainless bracket — feel it lock as you push it home by hand.

Hook up the throttle cable — 29:38

  1. 29:46 — The cable routes under the other cable, under the fuel injector, through the clamp, onto the drum. Use a small draw wire and remove the gear cover to watch it — routed wrong, it ends up in the starter gears.
  2. 30:24 — Correct position: cable on the engine side of the stainless tab, on the crankcase, inside the black spacer on the engine mount, sitting up high away from the gears.
  3. 32:32 — Drop the cable end into the throttle drum (no kink), pull the slack out, fit the cap/clamp.
  4. 33:26 — Set the adjustment: full throttle with a little play — no play, back off a touch; let it settle, then just nip the clamp — too tight jams the cable and it won't return. Confirm it's smooth.

Refit the fuel tank — 34:50

  1. 34:56 — Breather tube in (white collar back, poke in, tug, collar outward to lock), tube behind the tank strap.
  2. 35:15 — Seat the tank on its rubber knob, do the Velcro. Fit the power connector first (it's underneath), then the fuel line.
Tip: the entire job hinges on not losing the breather tube or the wire inside the board. Tie and tape everything to the board before you pull, tape a real ramp onto the plug so it can't hang up and strip, and pull the duck's-bill ducting out first — threading past it is where people give up.
Caution: order the hand control for your exact model (plug count and layout differ) — the wrong one won't function. Route the throttle cable engine-side of the stainless tab and up away from the starter gears, or it chews itself up. Nip the cable clamp only — an over-tight clamp stops the throttle returning. And lubricate the replacement cable every 6–12 months so you're not doing this again.
